Cedar Rapids, Iowa. February. A county road runs straight between snow fields until it reaches a corn wet-mill on the river, steam standing off the stacks in the cold. Six forty in the morning. In the plant's east electrical room, under one failed fluorescent tube, two contract engineers are looking at a motor control cabinet, and the cabinet has just tripped for the fortieth time in ten months.

[ADA]
There it goes. Right on schedule.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
It is not on schedule. It is on Tuesdays.

[ADA]
That's what I said. C-14 trips most Tuesdays, first shift, cold mornings. The plant knows. The plant does not care. We are here for the historian upgrade, not for their conveyor.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Somebody reset it forty times without asking it one question. That bothers me more than the trip.

[NARRATOR]
A man comes across the floor in insulated coveralls, hard hat under his arm, lunch pail in his hand. Third shift, going home late. The badge on his chest says Sowicki.

[GARY]
You looking at fourteen? Don't bother. She's done that since before my time. Moisture gets in the peckerhead, cold morning, she lets go. You get used to it.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Why?

[GARY]
Why what?

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Why does everyone believe it is moisture? Has anyone opened the connection box and found water?

[GARY]
Not that I ever heard. It's just what gets said. It's how she works.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
That is not a reason. That is a habit with seniority.

[GARY]
Ha. Well. She's all yours, buddy. I'm eleven hours into an eight-hour shift — Kowalczyk's kid had a thing at church, I took his back half. Hey, you're the one on the work order, right? F-something Flaukowski — is that an initial or a smudge?

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Flaukowski is plenty.

[GARY]
Suit yourself. Tell the day guys the west scale is reading light again.

[NARRATOR]
He waves without turning around and is gone through the roll door into the snow. Flaukowski already has the thermal camera out.

[ADA]
We are not paid for this.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
The camera was already in the truck.

[NARRATOR]
Forty minutes. The connection box is dry and has always been dry. What the camera finds is a current transformer reading one phase of a motor fed from a variable frequency drive, and the drive's switching noise beating against the overload's sample rate. On cold mornings the motor draws long and slow, the beat lines up, and the protection sees a phantom.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
There. It is not protecting the motor. It is protecting itself from arithmetic.

[ADA]
So write it up. Recommend they move the CT upstream of the filter, retune the overload class, vendor reviews it in April, maybe.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Or I change two parameters now and the plant stops losing an hour of throughput every Tuesday.

[ADA]
It's not our system.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
It is nobody's system, Ada. That is the problem. Everyone here operates it and no one owns it. Watch the amps for me.

[NARRATOR]
He makes the change. He racks the breaker back in and stands clear.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Closing C-14.

[NARRATOR]
The contactor pulls in. The conveyor rolls. The amps climb, settle, and hold flat.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Closed. Loaded. Holding.

[ADA]
Ten months of Tuesdays. You know what the plant will say. They'll say the weather turned.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
The weather gets credit for most of my work. The weather and April vendors.

[NARRATOR]
That should have been the end of it. A nuisance trip, a stubborn man, an ordinary fix. It would have been, except that documentation is documentation, and Flaukowski does his.

[ADA]
You're pulling event records? Now?

[FLAUKOWSKI]
The fix goes in the report. The report gets the record. Ninety seconds.

[NARRATOR]
The feeder protecting that section of the plant is a relay the size of a telephone book, and it keeps a diary. Every event, time-stamped to the millisecond. Flaukowski scrolls the file the way other men read box scores. Then he stops scrolling.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Ada.

[ADA]
We are leaving.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Look at the fourteenth. The ground fault on the east feeder — the one that took out the pellet line for an afternoon. Look at the sequence.

[ADA]

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Say it.

[ADA]
Operation logged at 06:12:30 point 850. That's the trip stamped three hundred forty milliseconds before the current. So the clocks are off. You know these logs, two devices, two clocks, somebody's GPS antenna is under a pigeon. Sequence-of-events files lie about order all the time. It's the first thing they lie about.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Both stamps are from the same relay. One device. One clock. It cannot skew against itself.

[ADA]
Then it's a firmware bug. Buffer writes out of order under load, events reassembled wrong, vendor errata nobody read. I will bet you lunch there's a known issue.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Take the bet. Then explain the waveform capture. The relay records the actual electrical waveform when it operates. The capture shows the trip signature — there, that shape — beginning, settled, confident. And the fault current arrives a third of a second later, into a breaker that has already decided.

[ADA]
Devices don't decide before the reason exists. So the record is wrong. Records are allowed to be wrong. It's the one thing they're reliably good at.

[FLAUKOWSKI]

[ADA]

[FLAUKOWSKI]

[ADA]
That's what worries me. You didn't say I won't be.

Monday night. Tuesday morning, strictly. A rented bay in an industrial strip off Sixteenth Avenue: workbenches, a salvaged server rack, two oscilloscopes, coffee cans of fasteners, one wall shingled in hand-pinned schematics. The wood stove is drawing well. Flaukowski has the event file up on three screens and has been checking clocks for four hours.

[FLAUKOWSKI]
Relay clock, disciplined by satellite. Checks. Plant historian, disciplined by the same signal, different antenna, different pigeon. Checks. My own capture from the maintenance port, time source number three. Checks. Three clocks. All of them agree with each other and none of them agree with sense.

[NARRATOR]
He speaks to the empty shop the way some men keep a logbook.

[FLAUKOWSKI]

[FLAUKOWSKI]

[NARRATOR]

[FLAUKOWSKI]

[NARRATOR]

[NARRATOR]
